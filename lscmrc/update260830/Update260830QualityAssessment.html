Quality, Not Quantity! A new way of recognising excellence at LSCMRC shows

For many years members have quite reasonably asked whether entering large numbers of exhibits gives an advantage over entering just a few — even when those few are of outstanding quality.

The Club has therefore been developing an additional, optional system that recognises quality of results, rather than quantity of entries. This system does not affect judging, placings, or existing awards. It simply provides a fairer way of recognising excellence over the course of a year.

How the Quality system works

Only first prizes count

Quality Certificates are based solely on first prizes. Seconds and thirds remain important for annual points, but this scheme focuses on outright success.

Strength of competition matters

A first prize gained against several different exhibitors is worth more than one gained where all exhibits belong to a single owner.

The calculation uses the number of exhibits and the number of exhibitors — figures already printed on prize cards.

Quality beats quantity

To prevent advantage through sheer numbers, only an exhibitor’s best five results at any show are counted. Entering more animals does not automatically increase a quality score.

Rare Varieties are recognised

Wins gained with officially designated Rare Varieties may receive modest additional recognition, reflecting the acknowledged difficulty of breeding and maintaining them.

Exhibitor-based awards

Certificates are awarded to exhibitors, not to individual mice or rats. This keeps the system practical, fair, and consistent across all shows.

A simple example from 2025

Exhibitor A — 4 mice entered

Several first prizes gained in competitive classes with multiple exhibitors, including one Rare Variety win.

Result: Quality Silver Certificate achieved.

Exhibitor B — 32 mice entered

First prizes mainly gained in lightly contested classes; additional entries did not increase score.

Result: No Quality Certificate.

What this shows

A small number of strong, competitive wins can now be properly recognised — without penalising anyone who chooses to show more animals.

What about Rare Varieties?

Each year the Mouse and Rat Show Secretaries compile a list of Rare Varieties. This list is informed by:
• objective evidence from the previous year’s show data (how often each variety was exhibited); and
• expert judgement, taking account of genetic difficulty, sustainability, and long-term conservation.

This approach helps identify varieties at risk before they disappear — learning from past losses such as Chinchilla.

A separate Variety Conservation Certificate recognises exhibitors who consistently support Rare Varieties over the course of a year.

Why this matters

This new system:
• recognises genuine excellence;
• encourages good breeding rather than mass entry;
• supports Rare Varieties without distorting judging; and
• provides a fair response to a concern raised repeatedly over many years.

Most importantly, it allows exhibitors who show a few really good animals to be recognised on equal terms.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Does this replace Championships?

No. Existing Championships and awards remain unchanged. This is an additional form of recognition, focused specifically on quality of results.

Does it affect judging or placings?

Absolutely not. Judges judge exactly as they always have. The system only interprets results after the show.

Is this compulsory?

No. Participation is automatic only in the sense that results already achieved are used. Members only need to apply if they wish to claim a certificate.

Does entering more animals disadvantage me?

No. Entering more animals is neither penalised nor rewarded. What matters is the quality of the best results, not the quantity of entries.

Why are Rare Varieties treated differently?

Because maintaining Rare Varieties is more difficult and vital to the long-term health of the Fancy. Recognition is modest and carefully controlled to avoid abuse.

This new Quality scheme does not change how we judge mice, nor does it replace any existing awards. It is simply a fair and thoughtful response to a concern that has been raised many times over the years: that quality can sometimes be overshadowed by quantity. By recognising strong, competitive wins — and by supporting Rare Varieties before they are lost — it is believed this approach strengthens the Fancy as a whole while remaining true to the spirit of LSCMRC shows.
